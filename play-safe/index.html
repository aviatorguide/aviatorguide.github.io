Play safe: the rules I keep for myself

Adults only, and only for fun

Every game on Entoto Skies is for adults aged 21 and over. I treat crash games as paid entertainment, like a ticket to a show: the money I put in is already spent in my head. No strategy, app or system guarantees a win, and the house keeps its share over time.

Warning signs I watch for

Problems rarely start with one big loss. They grow quietly, so I check myself against a short list from time to time.

  • You play with money meant for rent, food, school fees or family.
  • You raise your stake to win back what you lost.
  • You play longer than you planned, or hide how much you play.
  • You borrow money or sell things to keep playing.
  • You feel tense, angry or low when you cannot play.

My budget and time rules

I decide my budget in birr before I open any game and I never top it up during a session. For example, if I set 200 birr for an evening, the session ends when those 200 birr are gone, win or lose. I also set a time limit, because fast rounds make an hour disappear before I notice.

Chasing losses is the habit I fear most. A loss is the price of the evening, not a debt the game owes you, and a bigger stake only makes the next loss bigger.

Tools that help you stop

Serious operators offer deposit limits, session reminders, time outs and self-exclusion in the account settings. If you feel you are losing control, use them, and choose a longer break rather than a shorter one.

Talking helps more than people expect. Tell someone close to you what is going on, and if it weighs on your sleep, mood or money, speak to a doctor or visit a health centre. Asking for help early is nothing to be ashamed of.

You can also go back to the Entoto Skies home page and stay with the free demos, where no real birr is at stake.
